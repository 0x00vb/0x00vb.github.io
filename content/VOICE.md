# Writing voice

Write like a person explaining a decision to another engineer.

- Start with the concrete problem. A timeout, an expensive query, a missing permission, a confusing result.
- Keep paragraphs short. Use headings when they help, not to force every piece into the same template.
- Use first person for a preference or judgment. Don't invent a production incident, benchmark, customer, or personal achievement.
- Give one example that can be inspected. Name the relevant state, boundary, unit, or failure.
- Keep the tradeoff. Remove the motivational conclusion and the paragraph that repeats the introduction.
- Prefer ordinary words. No em dashes, dramatic framing, generic promises, or repeated “not X, but Y” slogans.
- Technical notes usually fit in 180-350 words. Essays can stay personal without turning into advice for everyone.
- A diagram should explain a mechanism or relationship. Keep the editable Excalidraw scene with the article assets. Label synthetic data as illustrative.
- Verify provider-specific behavior against primary documentation. Avoid hardcoded model prices, cache limits, or performance claims unless the article needs and sources them.

The archive date and URL remain stable during edits. New articles use their actual drafting and publication dates.
